Every Good Regulator

Why the systems that run our work fail — and how to build ones that don't.

Muhammad Hammad Shakeel

Every organisation builds a second system on top of its work: the dashboards, the platforms, the reviews, the compliance process — and now the AI agents that write the code and the pipelines that check it. When that layer goes wrong, it rarely breaks loudly. It keeps reporting green while the forest dies.

This book asks one question, again and again: what does the system that runs your system actually know — and who is allowed to tell it it's wrong?

Five words

01

Model

Anything that governs a system carries a picture of it. When the picture is wrong, it pushes the wrong way — hard.

02

Legibility

To govern something you have to see it, and to see it you simplify it. What the picture leaves out gets managed away.

03

Remainder

Automation takes the easy part of a job and leaves people the hardest part — with the least practice.

04

Stop

The most important question about any governing layer: who may halt it, and what happens to them when they do.

05

Jump

Layers stack: product, factory, platform, agents. Each jump creates a layer somebody has to govern.
