# Every Good Regulator — back cover and description

## Back-cover copy (about 150 words)

**Every organisation builds a second system on top of its work — and almost nobody designs it.**

The dashboards. The platforms. The performance reviews. The compliance process. And now, the AI agents that write the code and the pipelines that check it. This is the system that runs the system. When it goes wrong, it doesn't break loudly. It keeps reporting green while the forest dies.

*Every Good Regulator* follows that layer through Prussian forests and Toyota assembly lines, NASA's lost spacecraft and the F-35's logistics software, Britain's Post Office scandal and the software factories of 2026. It is built on five ideas — **Model, Legibility, Remainder, Stop, Jump** — and it gives you five questions to ask of any system that governs your work, from a company's operating model to your own to-do list.

With comics, haikus, "What If?" interludes and an original analysis of 240 real outages.

## Short description (about 60 words)

A book about the systems that run our systems — dashboards, platforms, processes, operating models and AI agents — and why they fail in predictable ways. Drawing on cybernetics, safety engineering and hundreds of real incidents, it offers five ideas and five questions for building governing layers that can see what they are doing, and can be told when they are wrong.

## One-line pitch

A book about metasystems engineering: why the machinery organisations build to run themselves fails, and how to build it so that it can be told when it is wrong.

## Selling points

- **A new frame for a problem everyone has.** Every reader works inside a governing layer. Few have a vocabulary for it; this book supplies five words.
- **Timely without being a hype book.** It takes AI agents and "software factories" seriously, dates every claim, and shows that the core lessons were published decades ago.
- **Original research.** A tagged analysis of 240 published post-mortems (Chapter 8), with the dataset released openly.
- **Primary sources, checked.** Myth-vs-Record boxes correct widely repeated claims against the original reports.
- **Fun to read.** Seventeen comic strips, four Exploded View drawings, four What If? interludes, four Dispatches from 2031, and a haiku at the end of every chapter — with humour kept out of the chapters about people who were harmed.

## Audience

Software engineers and engineering leaders; platform, reliability and AI teams; managers and operations people in any organisation that runs on dashboards and processes; students of systems thinking and safety; and anyone who has ever maintained a productivity system instead of doing the work.
